\begin{center}
{\large \bf Abstract \\ \vskip 1em}
\vskip 1em
\end{center}
\singlespace
\doublespace

This thesis investigates inference-time control of a pretrained latent diffusion model for single-image motion editing. The ML stack consists of Stable Diffusion v1.4, its AutoencoderKL image representation, a frozen CLIP text encoder, a conditional U-Net denoiser, and pretrained RAFT optical-flow estimation. Rather than training new weights, the method differentiates a motion-and-appearance energy through RAFT and the VAE decoder to modify the DDIM latent trajectory.

Translation, scale, stretch, and rotation parameters are converted into dense target flows that supervise the RAFT-based loss. At each denoising step, classifier-free text guidance predicts noise, the estimated clean latent is decoded, RAFT predicts source-to-output motion, and the flow and color losses are back-propagated to the latent. The implementation also uses mask-dependent gradient weights, gradient clipping, a guidance schedule, and RePaint-style latent replacement outside the edit region.

The evaluation reports a focused apple ablation with five method variants and three random seeds. The results show that RAFT-guided diffusion reduces final flow loss from 8.46 to 5.80 and reduces the best recorded flow loss from 8.16 to 3.07 compared with a no-RAFT diffusion variant. This provides measurable evidence that RAFT contributes to the pre-composite diffusion trajectory. However, the visually strongest final apple result is produced after deterministic restoration and source-object compositing, so the final image should be interpreted as a hybrid rendering result rather than an isolated diffusion output.

For large rigid translation, the system additionally encodes a geometrically initialized image into the diffusion latent space. Restoration and compositing address disoccluded background after sampling; for contained translations, the final saved image is therefore a hybrid result rather than a pure diffusion output. The contribution is an implemented and auditable inference-time ML pipeline with a limited but reproducible case-study ablation. Broader quantitative evaluation across additional objects, backgrounds, motion magnitudes, masks, and hyperparameters remains future work.
